The two methods compared

Nightly check (today) - utilities read flows once per night, between 02:00 and 04:00, when real demand is near zero. Leftover flow means leakage. Trusted, but it looks at the network only once a day, so a burst can run for half a day unseen - and a slow leak may never stand out.

Continuous twin (LeakGuard) - the app learns the normal pressure of every district for every hour of the day, from leak-free data. Every 15 minutes it compares live pressures with normal. When several districts dip together in a way normal operation never shows, it raises an alert and names the likely zone.

Where the data comes from

Simulated with the official EPANET engine on the EPA Net3 benchmark network: 92 junctions + 2 reservoirs + 3 tanks (all drawn on the map), 14 days at 15-minute steps. Leaks are modeled as pressure-driven orifices. Detectors are trained only on leak-free days. All scores are measured against the known truth, not estimated.
